Provenance · 48.7452° N, 4.0142° W

One coast, one licence, one cut a year.

Everything in the catalogue comes from a four-kilometre stretch of water off Île de Batz, and from a building two hundred metres from the slipway.

The water

Cold, and slow, and not especially hospitable.

The channel here holds between nine and thirteen degrees all year. Nothing grows quickly in it. A sugar kelp blade that would take a season in warmer water takes two here, and it spends that time building the polysaccharides it needs to keep its own surface intact against salt and abrasion.

That slowness is the whole reason we are here. The extracts we take out of this water are more concentrated than the same species harvested further south, and we can show it on a chromatogram rather than assert it on a box.

We hold a licence for four kilometres of it. We have never applied to extend that, and the working assumption inside the house is that we never will.

The harvest

Cut above the holdfast, in September, by hand.

Divers take the blade and leave the holdfast — the anchoring base the plant regrows from. Machine harvesting takes the whole organism, which is faster, cheaper, and finished after two seasons. Ours is none of the first three and not finished after nine years.

Every metre worked is logged with coordinates and depth, and the log is what the depth on the box is drawn from. When a stand looks thin, we skip it and the formula it feeds runs out. That has happened twice, most recently to the night balm in 2023.

The laboratory

Forty-eight hours from the cut to cold filtration.

Marine actives oxidise fast once they leave the water. Most houses ship dried biomass to a contract manufacturer and lose the volatile fraction on the way. We built the lab next to the slipway instead.

The house

Eleven people, four of whom dive.

Small enough that the person who cut the kelp and the person who wrote the pH onto the batch sheet still eat lunch together.

What we will not do
Grow faster than the kelp does.

No second cut, no second coast, no third-party filling. If demand passes supply, the formula goes out of stock and stays there until September.
